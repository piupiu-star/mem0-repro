## Table 4 - modification A (extraction disabled) paired test

| cutoff | n pairs | baseline right | modified right | flips | delta pp | 95% CI pp | McNemar p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| top_10 | 120 | 74 | 76 | 8 gained / 6 lost | +1.7 | [-4.2, +7.5] | 0.791 |
| top_200 | 360 | 235 | 279 | 51 gained / 7 lost | +12.2 | [+8.3, +16.4] | 0.000 |

A paired test is used because both configurations answer the same questions.
The p-value is an exact McNemar test on the discordant pairs only, so it
reflects how often the two configurations actually disagree -- not the raw gap.

`n pairs` is **not** the number of questions. A row pools every trial
available at that depth, and those trials are repeats of the same question
set. Pooling raises `n` and makes the p-value optimistic, so the per-trial
breakdown below is the conservative reading.
